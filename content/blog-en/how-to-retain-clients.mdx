Measure repeat visits using a group of people, not booking counts alone. Ten bookings could come from two regulars or ten different clients. Define the question before comparing results.

## Choose a group and period

Compare clients receiving the same type of service. A haircut has a different return interval from a one-off consultation. Record the initial month, expected return window and review date. [Client records](/en/features/client-crm) connect contacts and visit history.

## Calculate repeat visits

**Return rate = clients from the initial group with another completed visit / all clients in that group × 100.** Illustration: 8 of 20 clients return before the review period ends, giving 40%. This is a calculation example, not a Jedro+ customer result.

Separate a completed visit from a future booking. Use the same criterion in every report and avoid counting duplicate records as different people.

## Prepare a useful invitation

Say who is writing, why another visit may be relevant and how to book. Illustration: “Hello Maja, it’s Salon Luna. If you would like a haircut refresh, choose an available time through our link. Best wishes!” Adapt timing and tone to the service and the client’s preferences.

[AI communication](/en/features/ai-communication) helps prepare messages; [online booking](/en/features/online-booking) provides a clear next step. Before enabling automation, review a test message and the selected recipients.

## Review without assuming causation

Track invitations sent, replies and completed visits. Account for season, prices and staff changes. A return after a message does not prove the message was the only cause. After the first period, change one thing: the group, timing or content.
