Start by separating a no-show from a cancellation. A no-show means the client does not attend at the agreed time; a cancellation means they communicated a change. Track both separately because they call for different responses. No setting alone guarantees a full calendar.

## Establish a baseline

Each week, record agreed appointments, no-shows, timely cancellations and late cancellations. Define what counts as late for your business and use that definition consistently.

**No-show rate = no-shows / tracked appointments × 100.** This is an illustrative calculation: 6 no-shows out of 100 appointments gives 6%. The same 6 out of 120 gives 5%. Compare rates and volume, not counts alone.

## Check reminder content

An [appointment reminder](/en/features/sms-reminders) should include the service, date, time and clear instructions for making a change. Send a test to your own contact and view it on a phone. Test an incorrect number and a reschedule after the reminder has been prepared.

## Make the next step clear

Clients need to know how to reschedule or cancel. [Online booking](/en/features/online-booking) and [Receptionist+](/en/features/receptionist-plus) offer different routes to the same calendar. During setup, book, reschedule and cancel the same test appointment.

## Change one thing at a time

Check contacts and sending times first. Then test different wording or an agreed way to change appointments. Compare periods of similar length and note changes in prices, staff or season. A better week does not prove that the reminder alone caused the improvement.

## Hold a weekly team review

Review the last five no-shows: was the contact correct, was the message sent, and did the client know how to respond? Choose one improvement for the next week. Use [client records](/en/features/client-crm) to connect information and check [pricing](/en/pricing) for message allowances.
